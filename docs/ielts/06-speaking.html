06 · Speaking#

Module 06⏱ 6 min readView Markdown source

Goal: band 7: speak at length, naturally, with flexible vocabulary. · Time: 15 min/day, 2–3 weeks focused

🎯 What you'll learn

By the end of this chapter you can:

  • answer Part 1 questions with natural, developed answers;
  • plan in one minute and speak for the full two minutes in Part 2;
  • discuss abstract questions in Part 3 with opinions, reasons, examples and the other side;
  • show band-7 fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation;
  • practise efficiently with recordings and self-review.

1 · The three parts#

📖 Definition · Format (11–14 minutes, face to face with an examiner)
Part Time What happens How to answer
1 4–5 min familiar topics (home, studies, hobbies, daily life) 2–3 sentences: answer → reason → example or detail
2 1 min preparation + 1–2 min talk a cue card: "Describe a time when…" speak for the full 2 minutes; prepare 4–5 keywords, not sentences
3 4–5 min abstract discussion linked to the Part 2 topic opinion → reason → example → other side / future

2 · Part 1: short, natural, developed#

✍️ Worked example · A one-word answer vs a band-7 answer

Q: Do you like reading? ✗ "Yes, I do." ✓ "Yes, quite a lot, actually. I mostly read popular-science books because they explain complicated ideas in an accessible way. At the moment I'm reading one about the history of mathematics, and I usually manage a few pages before bed."

⚠️ Common mistake · Don't give speeches

Part 1 answers should be 15–25 seconds. Long memorised monologues sound unnatural and the examiner will interrupt to move on.

3 · Part 2: the long turn#

💡 Key idea · A structure that fits any cue card
  1. Set the scene (≈ 20 s): what, who, when, where.
  2. The story or details (≈ 60 s): follow the bullet points on the card.
  3. Feelings and reflection (≈ 30 s): why it mattered, how you feel about it now.
  4. If you still have time: a comparison ("Compared with…") or a hypothetical ("If I could do it again, I'd…").
✍️ Worked example · A cue card, planned and answered

Describe a teacher who influenced you. You should say: who the teacher was, what subject they taught, what they did that was special, and explain why they influenced you.

1-minute notes: Ms Lee · physics, grade 10 · experiments from everyday objects · questions not answers · olympiad

Opening: "I'd like to talk about Ms Lee, who taught me physics when I was in tenth grade. She was quite young at the time, and she'd only just joined our school…" Details: "What made her lessons special was that she built almost every experiment out of everyday objects. I remember one lesson when she used a plastic bottle and a bicycle pump to show us how rockets work, and the whole class ended up outside, launching bottles across the football pitch. She also had a habit of answering our questions with another question, which was frustrating at first but forced us to think things through ourselves…" Reflection: "Looking back, she's the reason I became interested in physics competitions. If she hadn't encouraged me to enter the regional olympiad, I probably would never have discovered how much I enjoy problem solving."

4 · Part 3: discussion#

💡 Key idea · Develop every answer

Opinion → reason → example → other side or future. Talk about people and society in general, not only about yourself. It's fine to say "It depends…", as long as you explain what it depends on.

✍️ Worked example · A developed Part 3 answer

Q: Is it more important for teachers to be knowledgeable or to be good communicators? "I'd say communication matters slightly more, at least in schools. A teacher can know a subject inside out, but if they can't explain it at the students' level, that knowledge doesn't really reach anyone. For example, many brilliant researchers are said to be quite poor lecturers. That said, at university level deep expertise becomes essential, because students need someone who can answer advanced questions, so I suppose the balance shifts as students get older."

5 · Band-7 moves#

📖 Definition · Fluency and coherence

Keep going: self-correct quickly and use natural fillers rather than silence ("Well…", "Let me think…", "That's an interesting question…", "What I mean is…"). Link ideas with a range of discourse markers: actually, that said, on the other hand, I suppose, as far as I know.

📖 Definition · Vocabulary

Topic words plus some idiomatic language used naturally: I was over the moon, it boils down to, a steep learning curve, off the top of my head. If you don't know a word, paraphrase ("the thing you use to…") instead of stopping.

📖 Definition · Grammar

Mix tenses naturally: past simple and past continuous for stories, present perfect for experiences, conditionals for hypotheticals ("If I had more time, I'd…", "If she hadn't encouraged me, I wouldn't have…"), future forms for plans. Aim for frequent error-free sentences.

📖 Definition · Pronunciation

Clear word stress (phoTOgraphy, photoGRAPHic), sentence stress on key words, natural chunking and intonation. An accent is fine; being easy to understand is what counts.

6 · Practice routine#

💡 Key idea · Daily and weekly
  • Daily: 3 random Part 1 questions + 1 timed Part 2 card + 2 Part 3 questions, out loud.
  • 2–3× a week: record a Part 2 answer, listen back, note 3 issues (long hesitations, repeated words, grammar slips), then re-record once.
  • Weekly: a mock interview with a partner or teacher who asks follow-up questions and interrupts like an examiner.

Practice (answers hidden)#

  1. Improve this Part 1 answer: "Do you live in a house or a flat?" — "A flat."
Answer

E.g. "I live in a flat on the eighth floor of an apartment block near the city centre. It's not very big, but it's really convenient because my school is only a ten-minute walk away."

  1. You have 1 minute for "Describe a place you would like to visit." Write 5 keywords.
Answer

E.g. Kyoto · temples + autumn leaves · history class · go with family · slower pace of life. Keywords, not sentences, so you speak naturally.

  1. Turn this into a band-7 sentence with a conditional: "I didn't study abroad. I am not very independent."
Answer

"If I had studied abroad, I would probably be much more independent now." (a mixed conditional).

  1. What should you do if you don't understand a Part 3 question?
Answer

Ask the examiner to repeat or rephrase it ("Sorry, could you rephrase that, please?"). That is allowed and doesn't lower your score; guessing at the meaning and answering the wrong question does.
