07 · Vocabulary & Grammar for Band 7#

Module 07⏱ 5 min readView Markdown source

Goal: the language range band 7 requires, used accurately. · Time: 10–15 min/day, ongoing

🎯 What you'll learn

By the end of this chapter you can:

  • learn vocabulary as collocations and phrases, not single words;
  • paraphrase by changing words and word forms, a skill used in all four papers;
  • build topic banks for the common Task 2 and Part 3 topics;
  • use the complex structures examiners look for, accurately;
  • find and fix the errors that keep most candidates at band 6–6.5.

1 · Vocabulary: collocations, not lists#

📖 Definition · Collocation

Words that naturally go together. Examiners reward precise, natural combinations more than rare words: make a decision (not do a decision), heavy traffic (not strong traffic), conduct research, pose a threat, raise awareness, a growing concern, widely accepted.

📖 Definition · The Academic Word List (AWL)

About 570 word families that appear across academic texts in all subjects: analyse, approach, benefit, concept, consist, data, factor, policy, significant, sufficient, …. Learn them in phrases: a significant increase, a key factor in, sufficient evidence to suggest.

📖 Definition · Topic banks

For each common topic, 15–20 collocations you can use in Writing Task 2 and Speaking Part 3: - Environment: curb emissions · renewable energy sources · carbon footprint · tackle climate change · sustainable development · biodiversity loss - Education: rote learning · critical thinking skills · tertiary education · a well-rounded curriculum · lifelong learning - Technology: a double-edged sword · the automation of jobs · digital literacy · data-privacy concerns · screen time - Health: a sedentary lifestyle · preventive healthcare · an ageing population · mental well-being - Work: job security · a work–life balance · flexible working arrangements · the gig economy

💡 Key idea · Paraphrasing: words and word forms

Change the words (grow → increase, important → crucial) and the grammar (grow → growth, economic → economy, rapidly → rapid). "The population grew rapidly" → "There was rapid population growth." You need this to read questions (03), understand audio (02) and avoid copying prompts (04–05).

💡 Key idea · How to remember it

Keep a vocabulary log where each entry is a phrase plus an example sentence you wrote. Review with spaced repetition (the flashcards tool does this). Reuse new phrases in your next essay or speaking practice: words you use are words you keep.

🎛️ Try it · Flashcards

Topic decks (Education, Environment, Technology, Health, Work, Government, Science, Society) plus Paraphrase and Linking.

2 · Grammar: range with accuracy#

📖 Definition · Structures examiners look for
Structure Example
Complex sentences (subordination) Although remote work offers flexibility, it can blur the line between work and home life.
Relative clauses Students who study abroad often become more independent.
Conditionals (1st, 2nd, 3rd, mixed) If governments invested more in public transport, congestion would fall.
Passive voice The data were collected over a decade. (Task 1 processes)
Nominalisation The rapid expansion of cities has led to… (instead of "cities expanded fast")
Cleft sentences What concerns many experts is the pace of change.
Modals for hedging This may/might/could lead to… · It is likely that…
✍️ Worked example · Upgrading a band-6 paragraph

Band 6: "Many people use cars. This causes pollution. The government should make public transport better. Then people will use it more." Band 7: "Because so many people rely on private cars, air pollution has become a serious problem in most large cities. If governments invested in faster and more reliable public transport, a significant number of commuters would be likely to switch, which would reduce both emissions and congestion." Changes: subordination (because), a second conditional, a relative clause (which would…), hedging (would be likely to), collocations (serious problem, reliable public transport).

3 · The errors that cost the most#

⚠️ Common mistake · The band-6 accuracy checklist
  • Articles: the for specific or already-mentioned things, a/an for one of many, nothing for general plurals/uncountables: "The government should improve ∅ public transport."
  • Subject–verb agreement: "The number of students has risen." · "A number of students have complained."
  • Uncountable nouns: information, research, advice, equipment, knowledge, traffic, evidence have no plural and take no a.
  • Comma splices: two sentences joined by a comma. Use a full stop, a semicolon or a linking word.
  • Tense consistency in stories and Task 1 (past data → past tense).
  • Prepositions: interested in, responsible for, depend on, invest in, an increase in sales of 10%.
💡 Key idea · Proofread with your personal error list

Keep a list of the 5 errors you make most often (from marked essays). Spend the last 3 minutes of Writing checking only for those.

Practice (answers hidden)#

  1. Correct: "The researches shows that the advices of doctors is often ignored."
Answer

"Research shows that doctors' advice is often ignored." (research and advice are uncountable.)

  1. Paraphrase using a noun phrase: "Prices rose sharply."
Answer

"There was a sharp rise in prices." / "Prices saw a sharp increase."

  1. Which collocation is natural: do / make / take a mistake, an effort, a photo, research?
Answer

make a mistake, make an effort, take a photo, do / conduct research.

  1. Fix the comma splice: "Many students work part-time, this affects their grades."
Answer

"Many students work part-time, which affects their grades." or "…part-time; this affects…" or "…part-time. This affects…".

  1. Combine with a relative clause: "Some cities have introduced congestion charges. These cities have seen less traffic."
Answer

"Cities that have introduced congestion charges have seen less traffic."
