Why these shapes

  
    
    
  • Warmup: at step 0 the weights are random and Adam's second-moment estimate is based on very few gradients, so full-size steps can blow up (this is the motivation behind RAdam, Liu et al. 2019). A short linear ramp (often 1–5% of training, or a few thousand steps for transformers) avoids early divergence.
    • 
    
  • Decay: a large LR explores quickly but bounces around the bottom of the valley; shrinking it lets the parameters settle into the valley floor. That's why the loss often drops sharply as soon as the decay starts (very visible with WSD).
    • 
    
  • Cosine vs linear: very similar in practice. Cosine spends longer near the peak and decays gently at the end.
    • 
    
  • WSD: constant LR for most of training, then a short decay. You can branch off a decay from any checkpoint of the stable phase, so you don't have to fix the total step count in advance (used for MiniCPM and several later LLMs).
    • 
    
  • Step decay: the classic ResNet/ImageNet recipe (÷10 at fixed epochs). Still fine for CNNs with SGD.
    • 
    
  • Inverse √t: from "Attention Is All You Need": lr = d_model^−0.5 · min(t^−0.5, t · warmup^−1.5). Here it's rescaled so its peak equals the peak LR you set.
