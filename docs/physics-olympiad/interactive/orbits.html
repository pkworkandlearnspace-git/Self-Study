Orbit Simulator

A planet starts at distance r₀ = 1 from a star (GM = 1). Set its launch speed as a multiple of the circular speed and watch Kepler's laws appear. The energy and angular momentum readouts should stay constant: that's your check that the numerical integrator is good (module 02).

What to verify

  1. Kepler II: the shaded sectors are swept in equal times, so they have equal areas. That's angular momentum conservation.
  2. Kepler III: the measured period matches T = 2π√(a³/GM), with a = −GM/(2E).
  3. Escape: at v₀ = √2·vcirc, E = 0 and the orbit becomes a parabola.
  4. Eccentricity: e = √(1 + 2EL²/(GM)²). At angle 0, the launch point is the periapsis (v₀ > vcirc) or apoapsis (v₀ < vcirc).
