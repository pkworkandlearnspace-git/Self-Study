Matrix as a Map

A 2×2 matrix is a machine that moves every point of the plane. Its columns are where the basis vectors land. Drag the tips of A·e₁ and A·e₂, or type the entries. The unit circle becomes an ellipse whose axes are the singular values.

Questions to answer with the tool

  1. Pick Rank 1. What happens to the circle? Why does det = 0 mean "not invertible"?
  2. Pick Rotation. Why are there no (real) eigenvector lines? What are the singular values?
  3. Pick Symmetric. Check that eigenvectors are perpendicular and match the SVD axes (true for symmetric positive-definite matrices).
  4. Pick Shear. Only one eigen-direction survives, but the SVD still gives two perpendicular axes. SVD always exists, eigendecomposition doesn't.
  5. Pick Ill-conditioned. κ = σ₁/σ₂ is large: the map squashes one direction almost flat. This is the shape of the loss valley that makes plain gradient descent zig-zag (see the optimizer landscape).
  6. Make det negative (e.g. Reflection). The orientation flips: e₁ → e₂ turns clockwise instead of counter-clockwise.
