Positional Encodings & RoPE

Attention is permutation-equivariant, so position must be injected. See the original sinusoidal encoding as a heatmap, then rotate query/key pairs with RoPE and check that the attention score depends only on the relative position m − n.

1 · Sinusoidal encoding (Vaswani et al. 2017)

Rows = position 0… (top to bottom), columns = dimension 0…d−1. PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / base^{2i/d}), PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(…). Low dimensions oscillate fast, high ones slowly: like the hands of a clock, or a binary counter made of waves.

Dot product PE(m)·PE(n) plotted against n − m for three different m (lines overlap): it depends only on the offset. Each sin/cos pair is a rotation, so PE(m+k) is a fixed linear function of PE(m).

2 · RoPE: rotate q and k by their positions

query pair, rotated by m·θᵢkey pair, rotated by n·θᵢ

RoPE splits a head's d dimensions into d/2 pairs. Pair i is rotated by angle pos·θᵢ with θᵢ = base^(−2i/d). The dot product of two rotated pairs depends on the angle between them, (m − n)·θᵢ, plus their original angle: only relative position survives. 🔭 In complex form: q̃ = q·e^{imθ}, k̃ = k·e^{inθ}, Re(q̃·conj(k̃)) = Re(q·conj(k)·e^{i(m−n)θ}).

3 · Long-range decay

Score q̃·k̃ for q = k = all-ones (perfectly aligned) as the distance m − n grows. The sum of many cosines with different frequencies decays in envelope, so far-away tokens get a smaller boost: a built-in locality bias. Raising the base (Llama 3 uses 500,000; Llama 2 used 10,000) slows the rotation, which is one ingredient of long-context extension.

Slowest pair's wavelength 2π·base^((d−2)/d)
