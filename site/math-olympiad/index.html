Full roadmap

Starting point: you've passed school/regional rounds and are strong at math. Target: national team selection, then IMO-level problem solving.

⚠️ Read first: eligibility and timing#

  • The IMO generally requires contestants to be under 20 on the day of the contest and not enrolled at a university. As a high-school senior, this cycle is probably your last chance. Verify the rules with your country's olympiad committee.
  • National selection often starts months before the IMO (July). Find your country's dates now and plan backwards from them.
  • The IMO paper: 2 days × 3 problems × 4.5 hours, 7 points each. Problems cover Algebra, Combinatorics, Geometry, and Number theory, ordered roughly easy → hard (P1/P4 easiest, P3/P6 hardest).

The map#

# Module Time You can…
01 Problem solving & proof writing 2–3 wk write rigorous proofs and use the core strategies
02 Algebra 4–6 wk inequalities, polynomials, functional equations
03 Number theory 4–6 wk modular arithmetic, orders, LTE, Diophantine equations
04 Combinatorics 5–7 wk counting, invariants, extremal arguments, graphs, games
05 Geometry 5–7 wk angle chasing → power of a point → projective/inversion → bash methods
06 Contest practice & solution writing ongoing sit full mocks and write solutions that earn full marks

Modules 02–05 run in parallel: rotate topics weekly instead of finishing one area before the next. Run module 06 constantly from week 3 on.

Interactive tools → interactive/#

  • Triangle centers explorer: drag a triangle and see the circumcenter, incenter, centroid, orthocenter, Euler line, and nine-point circle live.
  • Modular arithmetic lab: multiplication tables mod n, orders, primitive roots, Fermat/Euler check.
  • Flashcards: spaced repetition for theorems, lemmas, and techniques.

Weekly routine (~10–12 h)#

Day Do
3 days Topic study: read one handout section, then solve 4–6 problems from it
1 day Hard problem: one problem above your level, 1.5–2 h, no hints for the first hour
1 day Mock: 3 problems in 4.5 h (from week 3), then grade yourself strictly
1 day Review: write up solutions cleanly, read official solutions, update progress.md

The golden rule: stay stuck for at least 30–60 min before looking at a hint. When you read a solution, write down the key idea in one line. That line is what you'll remember and reuse.
