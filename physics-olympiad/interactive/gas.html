Kinetic Gas Lab

Hard disks bounce elastically in a box. Every disk starts with the same speed, yet collisions quickly spread the speeds into the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. Meanwhile the wall impacts give a pressure you can compare with the ideal-gas law (module 03). This is a 2D gas: 2 degrees of freedom, so ⟨½mv²⟩ = kT.

What to look for

  1. Relaxation: the histogram starts as a spike at v₀ and becomes f(v) = (mv/kT) e−mv²/2kT (2D) within a few collision times. Total kinetic energy never changes, only its distribution does.
  2. Ideal-gas law: measured P·A ≈ NkT for small disks. Raise r and the pressure rises above NkT/A, because the disks' own area is excluded. To first order P A/(NkT) ≈ 1 + nB₂ with B₂ = πd²/2 for hard disks of diameter d = 2r (the van der Waals "b" term). The estimate also uses A′ = (L − 2r)², the area that disk centres can actually reach.
  3. Mean free path: the dilute 2D result is λ = 1/(2√2 n d) (a disk sweeps a strip of width 2d, and the √2 comes from relative motion). The 3D analogue is 1/(√2 nσ) with σ = πd². The measured value comes out a little shorter, because finite disk size raises the collision rate (the Enskog factor; for hard disks it is about (1 − 7φ/16)/(1 − φ)² ≈ 1.08 at area fraction φ = 0.05).
  4. Heat ×1.2: every speed scales by 1.2, so T scales by 1.44 and P by 1.44, at constant N and A (isochoric).
Practice: 2D vs 3D speed averages

In 2D with m = k = 1: the most probable speed is vp = √T, the mean speed is ⟨v⟩ = √(πT/2) ≈ 1.253√T, and the rms speed is vrms = √(2T) ≈ 1.414√T.

In 3D the same three are √(2kT/m), √(8kT/πm) and √(3kT/m). Their ratios are 1 : 1.128 : 1.225. Check the 2D values against the readouts once the histogram has settled.
