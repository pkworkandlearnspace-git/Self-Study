Orbit Simulator

A planet starts at distance r₀ = 1 from a star (GM = 1, so the circular speed at r₀ is 1). Set its launch speed and angle and watch Kepler's laws appear. The dashed curve is the conic predicted from E and L at launch; the orange trail is the simulated path. If the two disagree, the integrator is at fault, not physics (module 02).

Every completed orbit is logged here. Try several speeds between 0.6 and 1.35. On log–log axes the points fall on a line of slope 3/2: T² ∝ a³, whatever the eccentricity.

Score: 0 / 0. Answers within 2% count.

What to verify

  1. Kepler II: the shaded sectors are swept in equal times, so they have equal areas. That's angular momentum conservation (dA/dt = L/2m).
  2. Kepler III: the measured period matches T = 2π√(a³/GM), with a = −GM/(2E). It depends only on the energy, not on the launch angle. Try angle ±40° at the same speed.
  3. Vis-viva: at every point v² = GM(2/r − 1/a). Watch the two vis-viva readouts agree.
  4. Escape: at v₀ = √2·vcirc, E = 0 and the orbit becomes a parabola.
  5. Eccentricity: e = √(1 + 2EL²/(G²M²m³)), which is √(1 + 2EL²) here. At angle 0 the launch point is the periapsis (v₀ > vcirc) or the apoapsis (v₀ < vcirc), and rp = a(1 − e), ra = a(1 + e).
Worked example: launch at angle 0 with v₀ = 1.2

E = ½(1.2)² − 1 = −0.28, so a = −1/(2E) = 1/0.56 ≈ 1.786. T = 2π a3/2 ≈ 2π · 2.386 ≈ 14.99.

L = r₀v₀ = 1.2 and e = √(1 + 2(−0.28)(1.44)) = √0.1936 = 0.44. The launch point is the periapsis, rp = a(1 − e) = 1.000 ✓, and ra = a(1 + e) ≈ 2.571.

Shortcut for angle 0: angular momentum and energy at the two apsides give ra/rp = v₀²/(2 − v₀²) = 1.44/0.56 ≈ 2.571 (in units GM = r₀ = 1).
