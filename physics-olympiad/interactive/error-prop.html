Uncertainty Propagation

Type a formula and the measured values ± uncertainties. You get the result with its uncertainty (independent errors added in quadrature), a breakdown of which measurement dominates, and a Monte Carlo check. The breakdown tells you what to measure better (module 07).

Use * / + − ^, parentheses, pi, sqrt, sin, cos, tan, exp, ln, log10. Angles in radians.

Rules

  • General: (Δf)² = Σ (∂f/∂xi · Δxi)² for independent errors. The tool finds each derivative numerically.
  • Sums: absolute errors add in quadrature. Products and powers: relative errors add in quadrature, each times its power, so for f = aⁿbᵐ, (Δf/f)² = (nΔa/a)² + (mΔb/b)².
  • If Monte Carlo and the linear formula disagree, the errors are too big for a first-order expansion (e.g. dividing by a quantity that is close to zero).
  • Round the uncertainty to 1–2 significant figures, and the value to the same decimal place.
