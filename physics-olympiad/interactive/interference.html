Wave Interference & Diffraction

Part 1: two coherent point sources make ripples, and you can see the nodal lines form. Part 2: the far-field (Fraunhofer) intensity of a single slit, a double slit or an N-slit grating with finite slit width, with a live phasor diagram that explains every maximum and zero (module 05).

1 · Two-source ripple tank

Nodal (dark) lines satisfy |r₁ − r₂| = (m + ½)λ for in-phase sources. They are hyperbolas with the sources as foci, and there are about 2d/λ of them. A phase lag Δφ on one source slides the whole pattern toward that source, because it needs a shorter path to catch up. This is how a phased-array antenna steers its beam.

2 · Single slit, double slit, grating on a far screen

Presets:
I(θ) totalsingle-slit envelope (sin β / β)²cursor: tap or drag on the plot

Phasor sum at the cursor. Each slit is split into 16 strips (small arrows). Inside one slit the phase turns through 2β, giving the single-slit curl. From one slit to the next it jumps by δ = 2α. The thick arrow is the resultant, and its length² is I/I₀. A minimum is where the chain closes into a polygon. A principal maximum is where every slit's chain points the same way.

I(θ) = I₀ · (sin β / β)² · (sin Nα / (N sin α))², with α = π d sinθ / λ and β = π a sinθ / λ. Principal maxima: d sinθ = mλ. Between two principal maxima there are N − 1 zeros and N − 2 small secondary maxima. A maximum goes missing when it lands on a single-slit zero (d/a an integer). Resolving power of a grating: λ/Δλ = mN.

Practice

1. Young's slits 0.25 mm apart, screen 1.5 m away, λ = 600 nm. Fringe spacing?

Small angles: Δy = λL/d = (600×10⁻⁹)(1.5)/(2.5×10⁻⁴) = 3.6 mm.

2. d = 3a. Which orders are missing, and how many bright fringes lie inside the central diffraction peak?

Order m sits at sinθ = mλ/d. The first single-slit zero is at sinθ = λ/a = 3λ/d, so m = ±3, ±6, … are missing. Inside the central peak (|m| < 3) there are m = 0, ±1, ±2: 5 fringes. Load the "Missing order" preset to check.

3. Can a grating with 600 lines/mm, 2 cm wide, resolve the sodium D lines (589.0 and 589.6 nm) in first order?

N = 600 × 20 = 12 000. Required λ/Δλ = 589.3/0.6 ≈ 982. Available: mN = 12 000 ≫ 982, so yes. Even N ≈ 1000 slits would do in first order.

4. Why is the N-slit minimum next to a principal maximum at δ = 2π/N?

N equal phasors, each turned by δ from the previous, close into a regular N-gon when Nδ = 2π. The sum is then zero. Set the cursor just beside m = 0 with N = 6 and watch the hexagon close. The angular half-width of a principal max is therefore Δ(sinθ) = λ/(Nd).
