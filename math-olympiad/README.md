# Math Olympiad: School/Regional → National → IMO

**Starting point:** you've passed school/regional rounds and are strong at math.
**Target:** national team selection, then IMO-level problem solving.

## ⚠️ Read first: eligibility and timing
- **Eligibility** ([IMO General Regulations §2.2](https://www.imo-official.org/regulations/)): you must be **born on or after 1 July of the year 20 years before** the IMO (for IMO 2027: born on or after 1 July 2007), and be **enrolled in full-time primary or secondary education on or after 1 December** of the year before the IMO (home-schooled students: no high-school diploma yet on 1 December). Teams have up to **6** contestants. As a high-school senior, **this cycle is probably your last chance**. Confirm with your country's olympiad committee.
- **IMO 2027:** Budapest, Hungary, **16–26 July 2027** ([imo-official.org](https://www.imo-official.org/)). National selection usually starts months earlier. Find your country's dates **now** and plan backwards from them.
- The IMO paper: **2 consecutive days × 3 problems × 4.5 hours**, each problem scored 0–7 (max 42). Problems cover **A**lgebra, **C**ombinatorics, **G**eometry, and **N**umber theory, ordered roughly easy → hard (P1/P4 easiest, P3/P6 hardest).

Modules 02–05 run **in parallel**: rotate topics weekly instead of finishing one area before the next. Run module 06 constantly from week 3 on.

## Interactive tools → [`interactive/`](interactive/index.html)
- **Triangle centers explorer:** drag a triangle and see the circumcenter, incenter, centroid, orthocenter, Euler line, and nine-point circle live.
- **Inequality lab:** power means, equality-case heat maps, tangent line trick.
- **Modular arithmetic lab:** multiplication tables mod n, orders, primitive roots, Fermat/Euler check.
- **Number theory toolkit:** step-through Euclid/Bézout and CRT, LTE checker, Legendre's formula.
- **Lattice paths & Catalan:** Pascal counts, obstacles, reflection principle.
- **Games lab:** Nim vs a perfect computer, P/N tables and Grundy values.
- **Which technique? quiz:** 22 classic problems; pick the tool that cracks each one (auto-graded).
- **Invariant & pigeonhole lab:** play erase-and-replace games, drag points in a square, tile a mutilated chessboard.
- **Flashcards:** spaced repetition for theorems, lemmas, and techniques.

## Weekly routine (~10–12 h)
| Day | Do |
|---|---|
| 3 days | **Topic study:** read one handout section, then solve 4–6 problems from it |
| 1 day | **Hard problem:** one problem above your level, 1.5–2 h, no hints for the first hour |
| 1 day | **Mock:** 3 problems in 4.5 h (from week 3), then grade yourself strictly |
| 1 day | **Review:** write up solutions cleanly, read official solutions, update `progress.md` |

**The golden rule:** stay stuck for **at least 30–60 min** before looking at a hint. When you read a solution, write down the *key idea in one line*. That line is what you'll remember and reuse.
