Also: points on ω are fixed; inside and outside swap; angles between curves are preserved; a circle orthogonal to ω maps to itself. Distances obey A′B′ = k²·AB / (OA·OB).

When to invert (olympiad heuristics)

  • Many circles pass through one point → invert there: they all become lines.
  • Tangent circles → after inversion at the tangency point they become parallel lines.
  • A product like PA·PB = const or a "sum of ratios" with lengths from one point → the distance formula turns it into a length statement.
  • √(bc) inversion: invert at A with radius √(AB·AC), then reflect in the bisector of ∠A. It swaps B and C, and swaps line BC with the circumcircle.

Practice (solutions inside)

1. Prove the distance formula A′B′ = k²·AB / (OA·OB).

OA·OA′ = OB·OB′ = k² gives OA/OB = OB′/OA′, and the angle at O is shared, so triangles OAB and OB′A′ are similar (SAS). Hence A′B′/AB = OB′/OA = k²/(OA·OB).

2. Ptolemy's inequality: AB·CD + BC·DA ≥ AC·BD, with equality iff ABCD is cyclic (in this order).

Invert at A with radius 1. By the formula, B′C′ = BC/(AB·AC), C′D′ = CD/(AC·AD), B′D′ = BD/(AB·AD). The triangle inequality B′C′ + C′D′ ≥ B′D′ becomes, after multiplying by AB·AC·AD, BC·AD + CD·AB ≥ BD·AC. Equality holds iff C′ lies on segment B′D′, i.e. B′, C′, D′ are collinear in that order. That line is the image of a circle through A, B, C, D in that order.

3. Show a circle orthogonal to ω is mapped to itself.

Let a line through O meet the circle γ at X and Y. By power of a point, OX·OY = pow(O, γ) = OT², where T is a point where the tangent from O touches γ. Orthogonality means that at an intersection point T of ω and γ the radii are perpendicular. So OT (a radius of ω) is tangent to γ, and the tangent length from O is OT = k. Hence OX·OY = k², so Y = X′. Every point of γ maps into γ.

4. (IMO Shortlist 2003 G4) Circles Γ₁, Γ₃ are externally tangent at P, and Γ₂, Γ₄ are externally tangent at the same P. Γ₁∩Γ₂ = A, Γ₂∩Γ₃ = B, Γ₃∩Γ₄ = C, Γ₄∩Γ₁ = D (all ≠ P). Prove AB·BC / (AD·DC) = PB² / PD².

Invert at P (any radius r). All four circles pass through P, so they become four lines. Tangent circles at P become parallel lines, so Γ₁′ ∥ Γ₃′ and Γ₂′ ∥ Γ₄′, and A′B′C′D′ is a parallelogram: A′B′ = C′D′ and B′C′ = D′A′. By the distance formula, AB = r²·A′B′/(PA′·PB′), and similarly for the other sides. Then AB·BC / (AD·DC) = [A′B′·B′C′ / (PA′·PB′²·PC′)] · [PA′·PD′²·PC′ / (A′D′·D′C′)] = PD′² / PB′² = PB² / PD², using PB′ = r²/PB and PD′ = r²/PD in the last step.
