Power of a Point & Radical Axis

Drag P, the circle centres, the radius handles (small squares) and the direction handle on the line through P. However the line turns, PA · PB stays the same: it is the power of P, equal to PO² − r².

Show

Try these

  1. Move P inside circle 1. The power becomes negative: PA · PB = −(r² − PO²) with signed lengths (A and B are on opposite sides of P).
  2. Turn on Keep P on the radical axis: the powers to circles 1 and 2 stay equal, so the two tangent lengths are equal too (Example 2 in module 05).
  3. Make circles 1 and 2 intersect. The radical axis passes through both intersection points: it is the line of the common chord.
  4. Turn on the third circle. The three radical axes always meet at one point, unless the centres are collinear (then they are parallel).
