Things to notice, then prove

  1. OG : GH = 1 : 2 always. O, G, H are collinear (the Euler line). Hint: the homothety at G with ratio −½ maps the triangle to its medial triangle.
  2. Euler's theorem OI² = R(R − 2r), so R ≥ 2r. When is it equal? (Try equilateral.)
  3. The nine-point circle passes through the 3 side midpoints and the 3 feet of the altitudes. Its center is the midpoint of OH and its radius is R/2.
  4. In a right triangle, H sits on the right-angle vertex and O on the hypotenuse midpoint. In an obtuse one, O and H leave the triangle.
  5. In an equilateral triangle, all centers coincide and the Euler line is undefined.
  6. Reflections of H (turn the toggle on): the reflection Ha of H over BC lies on the circumcircle, and the reflection A′ of H over the midpoint of BC is the antipode of A.
  7. Incenter–excenter lemma: MAB = MAI = MAC = MAIA, so B, I, C, IA lie on a circle centered at the arc midpoint MA, and IAI is its diameter.

Prove it (hints → full sketches)

OG : GH = 1 : 2

The homothety h centered at G with ratio −½ sends A, B, C to the midpoints of BC, CA, AB. It sends the altitude from A to the line through the midpoint of BC perpendicular to BC, which is the perpendicular bisector of BC. So h maps the altitudes' intersection H to the perpendicular bisectors' intersection O: O = h(H), i.e. O − G = −½(H − G). That is the Euler line with GH = 2·OG. In vectors with O as origin, this is H = A + B + C.

Ha lies on the circumcircle

Let the altitudes from B and C meet the opposite sides at E and F. In the cyclic quadrilateral AFHE (right angles at E and F), ∠BHC = ∠FHE = 180° − A. Reflection over BC preserves angles, so ∠BHaC = 180° − A. Then A and Ha are on opposite sides of BC with ∠BAC + ∠BHaC = 180°, so ABHaC is cyclic. (Use directed angles to cover the obtuse case.)

Reflection over the midpoint is the antipode

With O as origin, H = A + B + C. The reflection of H over M = (B + C)/2 is 2M − H = −A, which is the point diametrically opposite A. Equivalently BHCA′ is a parallelogram: BH ⟂ AC and CA′ ∥ BH give ∠ACA′ = 90°.

Incenter–excenter lemma

Let ∠A = 2α, ∠B = 2β, ∠C = 2γ. AI bisects ∠A, so it meets the circumcircle again at the midpoint MA of arc BC. Then ∠IBMA = ∠IBC + ∠CBMA = β + ∠CAMA = β + α. The exterior angle of triangle ABI at I gives ∠BIMA = ∠BAI + ∠ABI = α + β. So triangle MABI is isosceles and MAB = MAI. In the same way MAC = MAI. The internal and external bisectors at B are perpendicular, so ∠IBIA = 90°. Hence IA lies on the circle with center MA through B and I, and IIA is a diameter.
