## Vocabulary: learn collocations, not lists
- Band 7's Lexical Resource rewards **collocation, precision and style awareness**, not rare words. *"Make a decision"* (✓) vs *"do a decision"* (✗).
- **Academic Word List (AWL, Coxhead 2000):** **570 word families** in **10 sublists** (sublist 1 = most frequent), chosen because they appear across many academic subjects but aren't among the 2,000 most common English words (*analyse, significant, factor, benefit, policy…*). Learn them in **phrases**: *a significant factor*, *implement a policy*.
- **Topic banks:** for each common Task 2 / Part 3 topic, 15–20 collocations, e.g.
  - *Environment:* curb emissions · renewable energy sources · carbon footprint · tackle climate change · sustainable development
  - *Education:* rote learning · critical thinking skills · tertiary education · a well-rounded curriculum
  - *Technology:* a double-edged sword · automation of jobs · digital literacy · data privacy concerns
  - *Health:* a sedentary lifestyle · preventive healthcare · obesity rates · access to healthcare
- **Paraphrasing skill:** synonyms + changing the word form (*grow → growth*, *economic → economy*) + changing the structure (*more and more people → an increasing number of people*). It's essential in all four skills.
- **Method:** keep a vocabulary log (in [progress.md](progress.md) or the flashcards). Each entry is a **phrase + your own example sentence**. Review it with spaced repetition.

### Confusable pairs that cost marks
| Pair | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| rise / raise | *rise* (no object) · *raise* (+ object) | Prices **rose**. The government **raised** taxes. |
| economic / economical | about the economy · cheap to run | **economic** growth · an **economical** car |
| fewer / less | + countable plural · + uncountable | **fewer** cars, **less** traffic |
| the number / a number | a specific figure · several | **The number** of cars **has** risen. **A number** of cars **were** damaged. |
| affect / effect | verb · noun | Pollution **affects** health. It has an **effect** on health. |
| percent / percentage point | relative change · absolute change | 20% → 30% = **10 percentage points**, a **50%** increase |

## Grammar: range + accuracy
| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Complex sentences (subordination) | *Although* remote work offers flexibility, it can blur the line between work and home life. |
| Relative clauses | Students *who* study abroad often become more independent. |
| Conditionals (1st, 2nd, 3rd, mixed) | *If* governments *invested* more in public transport, congestion *would* fall. |
| Passive voice | The data *were collected* over a decade. (Task 1 processes) |
| Nominalization | *The rapid expansion of* cities has led to… (instead of "cities expanded fast") |
| Cleft sentences | *What* concerns many experts *is* the pace of change. |
| Modal verbs for hedging | This *may/might/could* lead to… · It *is likely that*… |

**Accuracy checklist** (the most common errors at band 6–6.5, all drilled in the [error hunter](interactive/error-hunter.html)): articles (*a/the/∅*), subject–verb agreement, countable/uncountable (*information*, *research*, *advice* have no plural), comma splices, tense consistency, prepositions (*interested in*, *responsible for*), word form (*dramatic/dramatically*).

**Range vs accuracy:** band 7 needs *both* a variety of complex structures *and* frequent error-free sentences. If a structure keeps breaking, use it less until it's reliable.

## Traps
- Learning single "impressive" words without their collocations → unnatural phrasing (*"a massive decision"* vs *"a major decision"*).
- Using informal words in Writing (*"kids", "a lot of stuff", "gonna"*). Speaking can be more informal; Writing should be neutral/formal.
- Overusing one linker (*Moreover, … Moreover, …*). Vary, and sometimes use none.
